About Me Hi, I'm Mahan — a self-taught, project-based learner on the path to becoming a full-stack web developer. Rather than working through theory-heavy courses, I chose to learn by building real, functional projects from day one, and that approach has shaped how I understand every concept I pick up. My journey started from the absolute fundamentals of JavaScript and gradually moved into more advanced territory: DOM manipulation, array methods, asynchronous programming with Promises and Async/Await, classes, modules, LocalStorage, and regular expressions. Each concept wasn't just studied — it was immediately put to use in something I built myself, which meant debugging real problems, not textbook exercises. So far, I've completed several full projects on my own: A task and habit management app — built with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, featuring input validation, dynamic categorization, persistent data storage with LocalStorage, and a fully responsive design across multiple breakpoints. A live movie and TV search application — connected to a real external API (OMDB), with live search handling race conditions, a recent-search history feature, hover interactions, and a glassmorphism video background design. An expense tracker with a custom donut chart — built entirely with CSS conic-gradient (no charting libraries), handling income, expenses, and running balance with a clean, color-coded transaction list. An in-progress online game store — currently building out an interactive image slider with autoplay and manual controls, dynamic product cards with clip-path based layouts, and hover-reveal overlays across dozens of product entries. Beyond just writing code, I care a lot about interface design — color, spacing, interaction states, and making things feel polished rather than just functional. I also make a habit of pushing each project a little further than what's strictly required, because that's usually where the real learning happens: debugging the unexpected, not the expected.

Vision for the Future My long-term goal is to reach a level where I can take an idea from a blank page to a real, usable product — entirely on my own, without needing to lean on anyone else to fill in the gaps. That's not about working in isolation; it's about building enough depth and independence that I can own a project end-to-end, make sound decisions about how to build it, and actually ship it. Right now, I'm focused on solidifying the fundamentals I've built so far — not rushing past them just to collect more buzzwords, but making sure the foundation is solid enough that everything I add on top of it actually sticks. From there, I plan to gradually expand into the broader toolkit that modern web development is built on, moving at a pace that lets each new layer genuinely connect to what I already know, rather than learning things in isolation. I don't believe in locking in a rigid, step-by-step plan years in advance. The tools and technologies I'll end up learning next will depend on what excites me, what I'm building, and what the projects in front of me actually demand — not a fixed checklist written today. Plans like that tend to look good on paper and fall apart the moment reality gets more interesting than the plan. I'd rather stay responsive to where the work actually takes me. What matters more to me than any specific sequence of technologies is staying consistent — building things regularly, hitting real problems, getting stuck, figuring my way out, and letting that process shape what I learn next. I've noticed that the skills that stuck with me the most weren't the ones I studied because a roadmap told me to; they were the ones I needed in the middle of building something I actually cared about finishing. So if there's one constant in how I think about where this is headed, it's that the clearest roadmap isn't the one you draw before you start — it's the one that takes shape as you keep moving, built one real project and one real problem at a time.
